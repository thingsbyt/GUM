# Claims and limitations

## Claims supported by frozen evidence

- GUM forms opaque event concepts from unlabeled pixel-transition evidence in the tested worlds.
- Those concepts support transfer under changed appearance and shuffled controls.
- GUM compiles successful concept sequences into persistent and composable skills.
- The same frozen concept-learning implementation controlled a verified real-file workflow.
- GUM evolved a task-acquisition strategy that improved held-out learning across most independent runs while retaining zero solutions.
- Major result packages are source-frozen and hash-audited.

## Claims not supported

- GUM is AGI, conscious, sentient, or alive.
- GUM understands arbitrary natural language, images, desktops, or physical environments.
- GUM invented its complete learning algorithm.
- GUM is the first system ever to learn concepts, skills, or learning strategies.
- GUM outperforms state-of-the-art meta-learning or reinforcement-learning systems.
- The current results will automatically scale to open-ended real life.

## Important experimental limitations

1. The environments were built by the project team.
2. The perceptual system assumes changed pixels are informative.
3. Concept features use learned pixel tokens but engineered distribution statistics.
4. The real-file world provides a quality display and five safe tools.
5. Meta-learning evolves only three supplied strategy features.
6. The strongest comparisons are against matched blank GUM copies, not established external baselines.
7. The latest meta-learning study has five independent runs; one regressed.
8. No outside group has reproduced the results.

## What would elevate the claim

- third-party task selection after the code is frozen;
- thirty or more independent seeds with confidence intervals;
- comparisons with random search, breadth-first search, PPO, model-based RL, and established meta-learning methods;
- ablations removing concept discovery, skill memory, composition, and strategy evolution;
- natural images and ordinary interfaces without a purpose-built dashboard;
- public reproduction on a different computer by someone uninvolved in development.
